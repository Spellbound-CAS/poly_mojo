# Division and greatest common divisors

Polynomial division separates a dividend into a multiple of the divisor and
a remainder. Gcds find a common factor that can be removed from two
polynomials. Both operations return structured results, with evidence you
can check separately when needed.

For coefficient and term inputs through `poly`, see
[exact array algebra](array-exact.md). This walkthrough uses the core types
and their explicit contracts.

## Divide with a remainder

Over a coefficient field, division gives

$$
f=qg+r,\qquad r=0\ \text{or}\ \deg r<\deg g.
$$

For $f=x^2+1$ and $g=x-1$, the quotient is $x+1$ and the remainder is $2$.

The result exposes `quotient` and `remainder`. `check_division` checks the

Keep those inputs if you will verify or transmit the result.

Ordinary division requires a coefficient field. For integer coefficients,
`pseudo_divmod` supplies an identity of the form $cf=qg+r$, where $c$ is a
power of the divisor's leading coefficient. The scale is part of the
returned evidence; dropping it would change the identity.

## Ask whether a division is exact

`divide_exact` accepts sparse polynomials and returns an optional quotient.
A present quotient means the dividend is divisible by the divisor in the
coefficient ring. An absent quotient means it is not. Invalid inputs and
work exhaustion still raise errors; they are not negative divisibility
answers.

For division by a list of polynomials, use `divide_by_list` with an admissible
monomial order. It returns one quotient per divisor and a remainder. The
remainder depends on the order and the divisor list; a Gröbner basis gives
it the stronger ideal-membership interpretation described in the
[Gröbner tutorial](groebner.md).

The gcd result contains the gcd and the two cofactors. Over a field, a
Bézout identity together with divisibility establishes that this is a
greatest common divisor. `check_common_divisor` alone establishes only the
common-divisor claim. Multivariate and integer cases use the appropriate
coprimality evidence instead of assuming that a univariate Bézout identity
is always available.

Normalization depends on the coefficient domain: field gcds are monic;
integer gcds retain their content and have positive leading coefficient.
The gcd of two zero polynomials is zero.

## Eliminate a variable with a resultant

`resultant` eliminates one variable from two polynomials; `subresultants`
returns the indexed subresultant family. These are useful for detecting
common roots and deriving equations in the remaining variables. The
[resultant theory](../theory/resultants.md) specifies the sign convention and
boundary cases.

The returned resultant identity proves membership in the ideal generated by
the inputs. Checking that identity alone does not prove that a proposed
polynomial equals the Sylvester determinant. See the
[certificate guide](../guides/certificates.md) and
[division and gcd reference](../reference/p2.md).
