# Exact real roots

These operations count and isolate distinct real roots using exact rational arithmetic.
Repeated roots count once. Sturm sequences retain the input and its derivative
without normalization or squarefree preprocessing; every division, including
the terminal zero remainder, has a quotient witness.

The root-count checker verifies polynomial identities and sign variations.
It does not run polynomial division or reconstruct the producer's sequence.
Nonzero constants use a one-element sequence and have no roots. Zero raises
`DOMAIN` for sequence construction, counting, and isolation.

`isolate_real_roots` isolates the roots of the squarefree part by Descartes'
rule of signs (see [Descartes certificates](#descartes-certificates)), on
an interval bounded by a power of two. Output intervals have nonroot dyadic
endpoints, a sign change of the squarefree part, and width below `2**-40`;
`refine` accepts any positive rational target width. It validates the
interval by Descartes' rule on the squarefree part, subdividing where the
bound overcounts, so any interval with exactly one distinct root and nonroot
endpoints is accepted, and narrows it by quadratic interval refinement to at
most half the target width.

To recover **all** rational roots, including non-dyadic values such as `1/3`,
isolation narrows every interval below `1/(2*A*A)`, where `A` is the leading
coefficient of the primitive squarefree part. The rational-root theorem bounds
root denominators by `A`; Legendre's continued-fraction criterion then finds
any such root among the midpoint's convergents. Each candidate is checked by
exact evaluation. Dyadic rational roots met at split points are exact
already. This is rational reconstruction after isolation, without integer
factorization. Rational midpoint roots remain strictly inside the open
interval returned by `refine`.

`count_roots` requires increasing nonroot endpoints. `sign_on_interval`
accepts singleton intervals and returns no sign if any root lies in the closed
interval. Budget exhaustion raises `LIMIT` and never returns a partial list.

## Descartes certificates

A `DescartesCertificate` proves the real roots of an integer polynomial g
without a Sturm sequence. Its leaves partition an interval (low, high) whose
ends are not roots. Each leaf records Descartes' bound for the roots inside
it: the sign variations of $(x+1)^n\,g(\ell + (h-\ell)/(x+1))$, which is at
least the number of roots in the open leaf and has the same parity, so zero
and one are exact counts. The shared leaf endpoints where g vanishes are
listed in `exact_roots`. Every leaf with a bound of one, and every leaf marked
`resolved` with a larger bound v, owns the next v brackets: disjoint
intervals inside it, each an open interval where g changes sign or a point
where g is zero. Each holds an odd number of roots, so v of them under a
bound of v hold exactly one root each. A leaf with a bound of two or more and
no brackets is unresolved and leaves its roots uncounted.

`check_descartes` verifies all of this from g and the certificate alone: the
partition, every leaf's bound recomputed by a Taylor shift, the exact roots,
and two signs per bracket. It does not need g to be squarefree.
`descartes_intervals` lists the certified roots in increasing order, an exact
root r as the point (r, r). A `DescartesRootResult` pairs a certificate with
the squarefree part g of a polynomial f, the cofactor gcd(f, f'), the bound
exponent of the certificate's interval and the outcome flags.

`descartes_count` counts the distinct real roots of f strictly between two
ordered nonroot endpoints from a complete isolation: the exact roots between
them and the brackets inside them, a bracket that contains an endpoint being
decided by the sign of g there against the bracket's end. Its
`DescartesCountCertificate` holds the isolation, the endpoints and the count,
and `check_descartes_count` verifies the isolation and recounts. It is the
evidence behind the array API's `count_roots`; `count_roots` here keeps its
Sturm certificate.
